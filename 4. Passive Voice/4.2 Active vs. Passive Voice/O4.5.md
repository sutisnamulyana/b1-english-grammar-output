Here’s a clear explanation suitable for a B1 English learner:

---

## **1. Active Voice**

**Rule:**
The subject does the action.
**Structure:** Subject + Verb + Object

**Usage:**
Use active voice when you want to show **who is doing the action**.

**Example:**

* **Active:** The chef cooks the meal.
  (“The chef” is doing the action of cooking.)

---

## **2. Passive Voice**

**Rule:**
The object of the active sentence becomes the subject. The verb uses “be” + past participle.
**Structure:** Subject + **be** + past participle (+ by + doer)

**Usage:**
Use passive voice when:

1. You **want to focus on the action or result**, not who does it.
2. The **doer is unknown or unimportant**.

**Example:**

* **Passive:** The meal is cooked by the chef.
  (The focus is on the meal, not the chef.)

---

## **Tip for B1 learners:**

* Active: who + does + what? ✅
* Passive: what + is/was + done (+ by whom)? ✅
* Common mistake: forgetting “be” or using the wrong verb form.
