### Omitting Relative Pronouns (B1)

**Rule:** In defining relative clauses, you can sometimes leave out the relative pronoun **who, which,** or **that** when it is the **object** of the clause. You **cannot** omit it if it is the subject.

**Usage:**

* Use omission to make sentences shorter and smoother.
* Only possible in **informal or neutral English**.

**Examples:**

* **With pronoun:** The book **that I bought** is interesting.
* **Omitted:** The book I bought is interesting. ✅
* **With subject:** The man **who lives next door** is friendly. ❌ (cannot omit “who”)

This helps sentences sound natural while keeping meaning clear.
