## **Omitting Relative Pronouns**

### **Rule**

You can leave out the relative pronoun **who, which, that** when it is the **object** of the clause.

* **Do not omit** if it is the **subject**.

### **Explanation**

* Relative pronouns connect two ideas.
* If the pronoun is doing the action, you **cannot** omit it.
* If it **receives** the action, you **can** omit it.

### **Examples**

1. **Object – can omit:**

* The book **that I read** was interesting. → The book I read was interesting.
* The man **whom I saw** was tall. → The man I saw was tall.

2. **Subject – cannot omit:**

* The girl **who is singing** is my sister. → ❌ The girl is singing is my sister.

### **Common Mistakes**

* Omitting the pronoun when it is the subject.
* Using "that" instead of "who" for people (optional for B1, but "who" is safer).
